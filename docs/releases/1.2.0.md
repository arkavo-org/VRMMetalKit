# VRMMetalKit 1.2.0 — opt-in material finishes and presentation features

1.2.0 adds a stylized glTF metallic/roughness finish, an outline minimum
width, a hair highlight, a contact shadow and crease AO. Every one of them is
off by default; with them off, rendering is byte-identical to the commit
before they landed (`18d6050`), except for the two behaviour changes below.

## Rendering changes since 1.1.2

`18d6050` is not `1.1.2`. Snapshots captured on 1.1.2 will move, so
re-baseline them. In the vrm-conformance suite every change that could be
compared against three-vrm moved toward it:

- **#429 convention audit.** MatCap and rotated `KHR_texture_transform`
  shading (matcap SSIM vs three-vrm 0.74 → 0.93 on the worst cell), capsule
  collider response, LookAt gaze, and the VRM 0.x parser.
- **#444 SpringBone push-out** no longer adds outward velocity. Every
  spring-driven cell moves. Synthetic chains change by an amount you can't see,
  but hair and bust on real avatars change visibly.
- **#445 synthetic collider sizing** (collider augmentation on): humanoid hair
  and bust settle differently.
- **Coming from 1.1.0:** 1.1.2's #427 warm-up fix changed every humanoid with
  spring bones. Hair hangs under its authored stiffness instead of freezing in
  the warm-up pose, and VRM 0.x colliders use the correct handedness.

## Behaviour change

- **`transparentWithZWrite` now writes depth.** VRMMetalKit parsed
  `VRMC_materials_mtoon.transparentWithZWrite` (and VRM 0.x `_BlendMode = 3`)
  but only used it for render-queue order: the surface never wrote depth. It now
  does, as three-vrm does. The visible effect is on outlined translucent
  materials: their own inverted hull, drawn after the surface, used to cover
  them almost entirely and now shows only at the silhouette. Outlines on plain
  `BLEND` materials (`transparentWithZWrite = false`) still cover their surface,
  as in three-vrm and UniVRM, where the outline drawn after a non-depth-writing
  surface covers it. None of the bundled AvatarSample/VRoid fixtures set the
  flag.
- **Single-sided `BLEND` materials cull back faces (#451).** Translucent
  materials used to render two-sided whatever their `doubleSided` flag. glTF
  2.0 and three-vrm cull back faces when the flag is false. With depth writes
  enabled, the back faces of a closed mesh hid its front faces in triangle
  order and caused stripes. Now a single-sided translucent surface shows only
  its front faces, both with and without `transparentWithZWrite`. Face overlays
  (eyes, brows, lashes, highlights) and lace/frill/ribbon/collar materials
  still render two-sided. Among local
  fixtures this affects AliciaSolid's translucent hair and Vita's `HairBack`. A
  material that relied on two-sided rendering should set `doubleSided: true`.

Because of this section, 1.2.0 shipped as pre-releases (`1.2.0-rc.1`,
`1.2.0-rc.2`) until vrm-conformance, Muse and GameOfMods had validated it.

## Highlights

- **Stylized metallic/roughness finish (opt-in).**
  `RendererConfig.enableMaterialFinish` reads each material's glTF
  `metallicFactor` / `roughnessFactor`: below `0.7` a broad sheen at dielectric
  strength (at most 10% of the incident light) in the base colour's hue
  (leather, patina), and from `0.7` up a tight, base-colour-tinted band (metal).
  Lower roughness narrows both lobes. It is opt-in because MToon, UniVRM and
  three-vrm all ignore the pair, and VRoid/UniVRM exports write the glTF
  default `metallicFactor = 1.0` on every material, skin and eyes included.
- **Outline scale and minimum width.** `VRMRenderer.outlineScale` is a direct
  multiplier where `1.0` is the authored width; the legacy `outlineWidth`
  (`0.02 == 1x`) composes with it. `outlineMinimumPixels` extrudes any hull
  vertex whose authored width projects thinner than the floor by the floor
  instead, on every hull path (single- and double-sided, rigid and skinned).
  The floor is scaled by `outlineWidthMultiplyTexture` and never adds a line to
  a zero-width outline.
- **Hair highlight (opt-in).** `HairHighlightConfig` adds a strand band along
  the material's UV `v` axis for materials whose names match (default
  `"hair"`). The band follows the base colour on dark hair instead of
  drawing a grey streak.
- **Contact shadow and crease AO (opt-in).** `enableContactShadows` grounds a
  skinned humanoid at its feet (following the skeleton) and other models at
  their mesh bounds. `enableSSAO` adds a derivative-based crease term to the
  indirect light only.
- **Material report.** `generateMaterialReport()` adds the authored
  `metallicFactor` and `roughnessFactor`, the `specularMode` the renderer
  draws, `outlineMode`, and `outlineWidthFactor`.
- **Benchmark.** `VRMBenchmark` (render and `--mode visionos`) takes
  `--material-finish`, `--hair-highlight`, `--ssao`, `--contact-shadow`,
  `--outline-scale` and `--outline-min-pixels`, and records the enabled ones in
  the JSON report's `config.features`. `PerformanceMetrics.contactShadowMs`
  reports the shadow's CPU encode time.

## Migration

- **These features change nothing unless you opt in.** `MToonLegacyGoldenTests`
  compares the default frame with frames captured at `18d6050` on rigid and
  skinned fixtures, including both outline hull paths, byte for byte.
- **Shader rebuild:** `.metal` sources are excluded from the SwiftPM build, and
  `MToonShader.metal` and `SkinnedShader.metal` now share `MToonShared.h`. Run
  `make shaders` after editing either.
- **Consumer pins (GameOfMods, MuseCore, SynthNPCKit, VRMAuthorKit).** The
  GameOfMods app pulls MuseCore as a remote package, so the bump takes two
  stages after this release is tagged and pushed:
  1. `scripts/sync_consumer_pins.sh 1.2.0` pins MuseCore, SynthNPCKit and
     VRMAuthorKit and re-resolves them. Release MuseCore.
  2. `scripts/sync_consumer_pins.sh 1.2.0 --musecore <new MuseCore version>`
     also pins the GameOfMods Xcode project to both and re-resolves it.

  Each stage runs all of its checks before editing anything; `--dry-run` stops
  after the checks.

## Verification

- `MToonLegacyGoldenTests`: default output byte-identical to `18d6050`.
- `MToonMaterialLayoutReflectionTests`: the Metal `MToonMaterial` seen by every
  MToon vertex and fragment entry point matches the Swift stride.
- `MToonFinishTests`: opt-in, three-swatch acceptance, dielectric sheen
  strength and hue, roughness narrowing, identical rigid/skinned shading, hair
  band visible from behind and following dark hair's hue, linear light scaling,
  smooth glossy band edges, silhouette suppression, material report.
- `MToonOutlineScaleTests`: minimum width on all four hull paths, width-mask
  scaling, zero-width outlines, 512 / 1024 / 2048 px readability.
- `ContactShadowTests`: footprint rules, a host-drawn floor, translucent
  surfaces, outline culling, clamping, and AvatarSample_U feet/hips tracking.
- `MToonBlendCullingTests`: a closed single-sided `BLEND` sphere renders
  pixel-identically with `transparentWithZWrite` on and off, and differently
  from its double-sided twin.

## Known issues

- Spring-driven hair can render differently from run to run when another
  process loads the GPU. Serial renders are byte-identical (#452).

## Measured cost

Host: Apple M4 Max (macOS 27), macOS FP16 shader slice.

- **Finish.** A full-viewport sphere at 4096×4096, median of 130 timed frames
  after 10 warm-up frames (Metal command-buffer GPU timestamps): matte
  `1.115 ms`, metal `1.368 ms`, **+0.253 ms (+23 %)**; three runs agreed within
  0.002 ms. Reproduce with:

  ```bash
  VMK_RUN_FINISH_BENCH=1 swift test --disable-sandbox \
    --filter MToonFinishTests/testFinishPerFragmentCost
  ```

- **Untouched materials.** With function constants on (the default), a
  material compiles in only the extensions it uses, so the others pay nothing.
  On the fallback pipeline (`enableMToonFunctionConstants = false`) compiling
  the extensions in cost a matte sphere `+0.08 ms` (+7 %) in the same setup.
- **Contact shadow.** One quad per frame; `contactShadowMs` reports its CPU
  encode time.

M1 hardware was not available, so the finish delta has no M1 row. Run the
command above on an M1 to add one.
